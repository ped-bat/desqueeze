01 — The squeeze

Anamorphic lenses record the world narrow

They fit a wider field of view onto the same sensor by compressing it horizontally. Straight out of the camera everything is tall and thin — circles come back as ellipses. Desqueezing restores the proportions the lens was actually seeing.

Squeeze factor

1.33× and 1.5× are the common still-photography adapters; 2× is the cinema standard. Desqueeze ships all three as presets, plus a custom field for anything in between.

02 — Lossless by default

Metadata, not pixels

Every other desqueeze workflow stretches the image. Desqueeze writes a tag and leaves your pixels exactly where the sensor put them.

The usual way

Resample the image

Stretching means interpolating every pixel onto a new grid. Detail gets invented where there wasn’t any, the stretch is baked in permanently, and you have spent quality before grading has even started.

Desqueeze

Write DefaultScale

Desqueeze converts to DNG and records the squeeze factor in the file’s DefaultScale tag. Not one pixel is resampled. Lightroom, Camera Raw and every other DNG-aware app read the tag and apply the stretch on the fly.

  • Pixel dataUntouched
  • ReversibleYes
  • Quality costNone

Need a flat file instead? Export to JPEG, PNG, TIFF or WebP and Desqueeze bakes the stretch into the pixels — with dual-illuminant DNG profiles handling the D65→D50 chromatic adaptation so the colour still lands where it should.

03 — The workflow

Drop. Set. Done.

Drop the files

Drag images or entire folders anywhere in the window — no drop target to aim for. Or browse, if you prefer.

Pick the factor

1.33×, 1.5×, 2× or a custom ratio, then choose your output: DNG for lossless, or JPEG, PNG, TIFF and WebP for a flat file.

Collect the results

Everything lands in a desqueezed/ folder next to the originals. Batches run in parallel, and nothing leaves your machine.
